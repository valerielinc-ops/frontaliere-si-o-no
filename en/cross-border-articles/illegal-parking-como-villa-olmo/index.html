Frequently Asked Questions
Can cross-border commuters with Swiss license plates be fined for illegal parking in Como?
Yes, the article clarifies that cross-border commuters with Swiss license plates are not immune from the rules of the Italian Highway Code. The local police of Como have sanctioned over 50 vehicles, including some with CH plates, for irregular parking, showing that the regulations apply to all vehicles.
How much does the Como-Lugano journey by car cost annually compared to the train for a cross-border commuter?
The estimated annual cost for the private car (fuel, motorway, parking in Switzerland, wear and tear) is around €4,600-6,900. The train (Trenord+SBB pass) costs significantly less, estimated at around €1,800-2,200 per year, making it the cheapest option.
What are the recommended parking lots near the border for cross-border commuters?
There are several options: Ponte Chiasso (CO) by train to Lugano, Gaggiolo/Stabio (VA) to Mendrisiotto, Luino – Ponte Tresa (VA) to Lugano by boat/bus, and Domodossola (VB) to the Canton of Valais. These parking lots offer affordable rates and reduce costs and risks of penalties.
Do the Como ZTL cameras also fine vehicles with Swiss license plates?
Yes, ZTL cameras read all license plates, including Swiss license plates. The fine for unauthorised access (from €83 to €332) is notified to the vehicle owner's address in Switzerland, thanks to bilateral agreements. Vehicles with CH plates are not exempt from ZTL regulations.
How can cross-border commuters avoid fines and mobility problems in the center of Como?
It is advisable to check the map and ZTL timetables, use apps such as 'Como Parking' to find affiliated parking spaces or prefer taxis/car-sharing for the center. For those who park outside the city, parking lots are an excellent solution to save money and avoid traffic, remembering that ZTL cameras also fine Swiss license plates.
